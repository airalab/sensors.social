Construction sites: data instead of complaints

An open network of Altruist sensors tracks dust and noise around building sites, day and night. Residents host the sensors, and everyone sees the same public map.

Why complaints do not work

The municipality sees snapshots

One or two measurements a month, so dust peaks go unseen.

Residents have no numbers

A complaint is subjective and carries no measurement.

Developers have no incentive

Without data, cutting dust and noise brings them nothing.

Why cities switched to continuous monitoring

London

A construction boom buried councils in complaints. Sites now monitor PM10 at their boundary.

Above 190 µg/m³ in an hour, the site investigates and stops dusty work if needed.

Singapore

Construction noise in dense housing. Developers monitor it at their own cost, with fines up to SGD 40,000 per offence.

Managing noise became cheaper than paying fines.

Seoul

Citizens demanded air data. The city installed about 1,100 S-DoT sensors with open access.

Decisions rest on facts, not impressions.

Hong Kong

Site incidents raised public pressure. The 4S smart safety system has been mandatory on large public works since 2023.

Sensors and automatic alerts on every such site.

Stuttgart

Citizens built Luftdaten in 2015 from DIY sensors at about EUR 30 each. Today sensors.community spans 70+ countries.

Citizen data pushed the city to driving bans.

The common thread

Public pressure started it, and objective data solved it. Luftdaten showed that a network built by citizens can grow faster and cost less than one bought from the top down.

An open sensor network instead of a contractor

No corporate cloud

Data sits on network nodes inside the city and is verified cryptographically, so past readings cannot be rewritten.

Open software

The municipality, developers and residents can all check what is measured and how. There are no hidden algorithms.

EUR 210 per sensor

Open design makes the network a public good, which corporate solutions cannot offer.

How a civic network starts

The municipality organizes, citizens are the network. It follows the sensors.community model rather than a B2B contract with a monitoring company.

  1. 1 The municipality knows where next year's construction will be.
  2. 2 It announces 10–20 funded sensors on its website and at resident meetings.
  3. 3 Residents near the site request a free sensor, online with a code or in person.
  4. 4 The sensor goes on a balcony or facade and joins Wi-Fi.
  5. 5 Data appears on the public map automatically.
Hardware for one network
EUR 2,100–4,200
10–20 Altruist Urban sensors at EUR 210 each

The municipality keeps a stock of sensors and hands them out knowing each address, so coverage lands right around the site. Data stays on a municipal server, with open-source software.

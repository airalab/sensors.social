## Why we went

Altruist usually lives on a balcony. We wanted to see it on the road: in cozy hotels, towering skyscrapers, fitness clubs and busy streets. Once you start measuring, it quickly turns into a hobby. Altruist shows particles, noise, temperature, humidity and pressure in real time, and every reading goes into an open, tamper-proof public record. So we packed a few sensors and filmed the trip as a series of short episodes.

## Bangkok: two streets, two different airs

<div class="grid">

![A blue Altruist with an orange roof on a kerbside wall, Bangkok traffic behind it](./images/bangkok-traffic.jpg)
![A pink Altruist on a column next to the Sukhumvit 33 street sign](./images/bangkok-sukhumvit.jpg)
![A pink Altruist with a turquoise roof on a wet table after the rain](./images/bangkok-rain.jpg)

</div>

The tour started in Bangkok. One sensor went next to Sukhumvit, the busiest road in the city, another into an apartment on a quiet side street five minutes away. Almost the same point on the map, yet clearly different readings and a different rhythm of the day. A city average fits nobody.

Then came the surprises. Next to a six-lane highway with a train right overhead, the air was cleaner than we expected. The megacity's air was pretty clean overall, but the main avenues were definitely loud. Altruist tracks noise and dust side by side, in real time.

## Koh Phangan: smoke in paradise

<div class="grid">

![A pink Altruist on a white wall above the jungle of Koh Phangan](./images/phangan-jungle.jpg)
![A pink Altruist on a bamboo wall](./images/phangan-bamboo.jpg)
![Live readings on a tablet next to a sensor above the jungle](./images/phangan-tablet.jpg)

</div>

Next stop: Koh Phangan, a true tropical paradise. We placed sensors in several spots across the island and let them run for days. Then someone nearby started burning waste. The sensors caught the rise in fine particles right away, and it showed up on the sensors.social map, so anyone nearby could react in time: close the windows, step away, raise the issue. After a few days of measurements we could say it: the experiment was a success.

## What we take home

- Air is local. A city average says little about your street, your floor or your room.
- Noise matters. Some of the loudest avenues had pretty clean air.
- Open data helps everyone nearby. When the data is public, anyone close by sees the smoke in time.
- Setup is easy anywhere. Every new episode ends with a QR code: scan it, pick your AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok) and it walks you through setup in your own language.

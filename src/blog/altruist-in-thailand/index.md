No numbers in this post. We took a box of Altruists to Thailand and photographed them where they actually live — on columns and kerbs in Bangkok, on a scooter handlebar in the rain, on a white wall above the jungle on Koh Phangan.

## At street level

A sensor is only honest about the air where it hangs. Ours spend their working life at roughly head height on a street: beside a traffic lane, under the skytrain, next to the awning you wait out a downpour under. That is the air in these frames — Sukhumvit, at the height where people breathe it.

## The hat is not decoration

The cone on top is the first thing anyone asks about, and the answer is dull: tropical rain arrives sideways. The roof keeps the water off while leaving the air path open the whole way round. It has a second effect nobody planned for — the device reads as an object rather than as equipment. A small painted house on a pillar, which is roughly what it is.

## After dark

Colour turns out to be the other half of the design. A grey box on a wall is infrastructure and nobody looks at it twice. A magenta one under shop lights gets asked about, and being asked about is how a citizen network grows.
